What is stored on this device now

Hunt has no account, no sign-in, no paid plan, and no server copy of your hunt. Hunt takes no payment and stores no card. What you save stays in this browser’s localStorage and IndexedDB until you export it, delete it, or clear site data. Hunt runs no analytics, no ads, and no tracking pixels, and sets no cookies. The service worker cache stores the app shell so Hunt can open offline. That cache is not a profile of you.

These are the localStorage keys the app reads or writes, and what each one is for:

  • hunt-tracked — items you track.
  • hunt-show-pay — whether Show Mode records sales as cash or card by default.
  • hunt-stock-v1 — a backup copy of the inventory tracker, used only when this browser blocks IndexedDB.
  • hunt-upc-proxy — optional. Only set if the owner saves a barcode lookup proxy URL on this device. Hunt does not set it on its own.
  • hunt-case-cost — the cost per car you type on Search by case, used only for the fee check on this device.
  • hunt-vision-proxy — optional, off by default. Only set if the owner saves an AI photo match proxy URL on Scan. While it is set, photos you identify are also sent to that proxy. Turn off removes it.
  • hunt-alerts — alert rules. If you type an email for a future email fallback, that address stays in this key on this device. Hunt does not send that email.
  • hunt-settings — marketplace fee percent, shipping and supplies, minimum profit, and minimum sales.
  • hunt-stores — stores you pick for stock alerts.
  • hunt-theme — appearance: system, light, or dark.
  • hunt-recent — items you opened recently on this device.
  • hunt-coupons — coupons saved on this device. Owner coupons are not published with the site.
  • hunt-owner — a flag that this browser turned on the owner coupon tools.
  • reseller-inventory-v2 — the inventory book: packages, cost, status, and sold history.
  • reseller-bought — an older inventory list. Hunt reads it once and copies it into reseller-inventory-v2.
  • reseller-books — an older inventory list. Hunt reads it once and copies it into reseller-inventory-v2.
  • reseller-ship — the shipping amount in the profit calculator.
  • reseller-tax — the optional tax amount in the profit calculator.
  • reseller-grade — the grade you set on a catalog item.
  • reseller-stock — on shelf, not here, or sold out marks.
  • reseller-sizes — the size you set on shoes or clothes.
  • reseller-shelf — a shelf price you type.
  • reseller-checked-at — the time of the last refresh on this device.
  • reseller-provider-log — the last source-check result stored on this device.

The inventory tracker and Show Mode keep your stock, item photos you add, sales, and a copy of Hunt's price comps in this browser's IndexedDB database named hunt-stock. It stays on this device until you export it, delete it, or clear site data. Nothing in it is sent to Shopify or any server; the Shopify CSV is a file you download yourself.

Who your browser talks to

Hunt is a static website. Opening it sends nothing about you to Broken Beaker Labs LC, because there is no Hunt server that receives your data. These outside services do receive requests from your browser, and each sees your IP address, browser type, and the time, under its own privacy policy:

  • GitHub Pages (barronstrikesgold-cyber.github.io) hosts the site. GitHub may log visitor IP addresses for security under the GitHub Privacy Statement.
  • Product photo hosts. Photos load straight from the shop or database that publishes them, with no referrer: for example tcgplayer-cdn.tcgplayer.com, www.lego.com, svgs.scryfall.io, store.storeimages.cdn-apple.com, cdn.shopify.com, static.wikia.nocookie.net, and Pokémon card image hosts. Some of these hosts set their own cookies; for example www.lego.com sets a Cloudflare security cookie (__cf_bm) on its own domain. Hunt does not read those cookies.
  • Barcode and card databases, only when you scan or type a code Hunt does not know (details below).
  • cdn.jsdelivr.net, only the first time you use Identify from photo (details below).
  • Links to Amazon, eBay, Google, shops, and sources open in a new tab only when you tap them. What happens there is under that site’s policy.

Barcode lookup (third-party product databases)

When you scan or type a barcode, Hunt checks its own catalog, price list, and your inventory on this device first. If the code is a retail UPC or EAN that Hunt does not know, the app sends only that barcode number from your browser to public product databases to get a product name, brand, category, description, and photo links: barcode.report, then Open Products Facts and Open Food Facts. If the owner saved a lookup proxy URL in hunt-upc-proxy, the barcode also goes to that proxy, which asks UPCitemdb. No name, email, location, or inventory data is sent, and requests carry no cookies. Like any website, those services see your IP address and the request time under their own privacy policies.

Product photos from a web match load directly from the store or database that hosts them, with no referrer sent. Lookup answers, and codes you confirmed, are cached in this browser's IndexedDB database named hunt-upc so a repeat scan is instant and works offline. When you add a matched product, its photo link and, when the host allows it, a small local copy of the photo are saved with the item in hunt-stock. "Search the web" buttons open Google, eBay, Amazon (with the Associates tag hempsply02-20), barcode.report, or UPCitemdb in a new tab only when you tap them. Delete my data on this device clears both databases.

Some Mattel barcodes cover a whole case (for example Hot Wheels Monster Trucks 1:64). For those, Hunt shows its own list of that case from files hosted with this site, so picking an item or typing its item code sends nothing to a third party. The case photo loads from Mattel's shop image server (cdn.shopify.com) and item photos from the Hot Wheels Wiki image server (static.wikia.nocookie.net), with no referrer.

Identify from photo

When you pick or take a photo on Scan, Hunt reads the text in it on this device. The photo is not uploaded. The free text reader (Tesseract.js) and its English language data download once from cdn.jsdelivr.net the first time you use it; that request carries no photo and no cookies. If the photo shows a barcode or an item code, Hunt looks it up the same way as a scanned barcode (above). If it shows a trading-card number such as 158/128, Hunt sends only that number and the card's name words to the Pokémon TCG API (api.pokemontcg.io), or to TCGdex (api.tcgdex.net) when that API is busy, to find the card and its picture.

An AI photo match is optional and off by default. It only runs if the owner saves a vision proxy URL on this device (hunt-vision-proxy). Then a smaller copy of the photo (768 pixels, JPEG) and the text Hunt read go to that proxy, which the owner runs and which forwards them to the vision service the owner chose, under that service's privacy policy. Hunt holds no key for it. Turn off on Scan stops it.

Items you confirm are saved to a learned catalog in this browser's IndexedDB database hunt-upc, so future scans, photo matches, and Search find them. Nothing from it leaves the device. "Suggest for the shared Hunt catalog" opens a prefilled GitHub issue in a new tab only when you tap it; it contains the product name, codes, photo link, and source link, and nothing is posted unless you press Submit on GitHub, where GitHub's privacy policy applies. A scheduled check then re-verifies the suggestion against public sources before it is added to the catalog published with this site.

Search text is not saved under its own key. Categories of information on this device are the identifiers you choose to type (such as an alert email), and your app activity (tracked items, alerts, inventory, and settings).

What Hunt does not collect

Hunt does not collect sensitive personal information, precise location, contacts, payment details, or biometric data. The camera is used only while Scan or Identify from photo is open, and camera frames and photos are read on the device. Hunt does not sell or share personal information, and does not use it for targeted advertising or profiling.

Planned accounts

Sign-in is not in this version. A later version is planned for people in the United States: Continue with Google, and an email magic link, using Supabase. Sign in with Apple comes after Broken Beaker Labs LC has an Apple Developer account. Guest use stays. You will still be able to use Hunt with nothing stored on a server.

When an account exists, the plan is to sync tracked items, inventory, alerts, and settings, plus a profile with email, an age confirmation, and whether you opted in to email. Recent items (hunt-recent) stay on the device. Owner coupon keys (hunt-coupons and hunt-owner) are not uploaded. A session token may then be stored in localStorage under a key that starts with sb-. That key is not written today.

Accounts will be for people 13 and older. Someone under 13 will not get an account, and nothing about that person will be stored on a server. Pro checkout, if it is offered, will be for people 18 and older and will use Stripe on the web.

Your privacy choices

Broken Beaker Labs LC does not sell personal information. Broken Beaker Labs LC does not share personal information for cross-context behavioral advertising. You will not be treated differently for asking to know, delete, or correct your information.

Amazon links use the Associates tag hempsply02-20. As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases. Hunt does not send your personal information to Amazon. After you leave Hunt, Amazon may set its own cookies on amazon.com. That commission link is not a sale of your personal information.

Hunt honors Global Privacy Control. If your browser sends the Global Privacy Control signal, Hunt treats that as an opt-out of sale and sharing. Hunt already does not sell or share personal information, so the signal does not turn on a separate tracker.

On this device, open My Stuff, then Settings. Export my data downloads a JSON file of every hunt- and reseller- key, plus the IndexedDB databases hunt-stock (inventory tracker, Show Mode sales, item photos) and the learned catalog in hunt-upc. Delete my data on this device asks you to confirm, then removes those keys and clears both IndexedDB databases. After that, Hunt keeps an empty inventory record so the starter book is not copied back onto the device.

Age

Hunt is for people 13 and older, and the footer of every screen says so. Under the Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA), Hunt does not knowingly collect personal information from a child under 13. There is no account to create in this version. When accounts exist, a person under 13 will stay in guest mode and nothing about them will be stored on a server.

Retention

Information in localStorage and IndexedDB stays on this device until you delete it or clear the browser. Broken Beaker Labs LC keeps no copy. Hosting logs, if any, are kept by GitHub under its own policy. Planned account information, once accounts exist, is kept while the account is active. A deletion request will be completed within 30 days. Backup copies, once a paid Supabase plan is in use, are planned to roll off within 7 days. An inactive account is planned to get a warning at 23 months and to be deleted at 24 months. Authentication logs are planned to be kept for 90 days. None of that account data exists yet.

Access, deletion, and correction

You can take a copy with Export my data, and you can erase this device with Delete my data on this device, both under My Stuff → Settings. For a future account, the public page Delete account explains what deletion will cover. You can also ask to know, delete, or correct information by email.
